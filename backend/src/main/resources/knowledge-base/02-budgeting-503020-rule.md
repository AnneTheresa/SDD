# The 50/30/20 Budgeting Guideline

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to think about splitting take-home income into three broad buckets:

- **50% Needs** - housing, utilities, groceries, minimum debt payments, transport to work.
- **30% Wants** - dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, hobbies, non-essential shopping.
- **20% Savings and debt paydown** - emergency fund contributions, savings goals, extra debt repayment.

It is a starting guideline, not a strict rule. Someone with high housing costs in an expensive city, or someone aggressively paying down debt, will naturally have different proportions. The value of the framework is in noticing which bucket is out of balance, not in hitting the percentages exactly.

How to use it practically:

- Review recent spending by category to see roughly how your own split compares to 50/30/20.
- If "wants" spending is consistently crowding out savings, look for one or two categories (like dining out or subscriptions) to trim rather than cutting everything at once - small, sustainable changes tend to stick better than large ones.
- Revisit the split periodically as income or expenses change, rather than setting it once and forgetting it.

This is general budgeting education. It is not a personalized financial plan and does not account for debt, tax, or investment considerations specific to any individual.
