# Building an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside specifically for unexpected expenses, such as a car repair, a medical bill, or a period without income. It is separate from money saved toward a goal like a vacation or a big purchase.

A common starting target is one month of essential expenses (housing, utilities, groceries, transport), then building up toward three to six months of essential expenses over time. There is no single "correct" number - it depends on job stability, dependants, and other safety nets available.

Practical tips for building an emergency fund:

- Start small and automate it. Even a fixed amount moved into savings right after payday adds up faster than trying to save "whatever is left over" at the end of the month.
- Keep it in an account that is easy to access without penalty, separate from everyday spending, so it is not accidentally spent.
- Treat contributions like a recurring bill you pay to yourself.
- If an emergency happens and the fund is used, prioritize rebuilding it before resuming other savings goals.

This is general education, not a recommendation about specific products, investment allocations, or how much any individual should save - that depends on personal circumstances.
