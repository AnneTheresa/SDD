# Savings Accounts vs. Everyday (Chequing) Accounts

A chequing/everyday account is designed for frequent activity - paying bills, debit purchases, transfers - and typically holds money that will be spent soon. A savings account is designed to hold money you are not planning to spend immediately, and often earns interest on the balance.

Keeping savings in a separate account (rather than mixed into the everyday account) has a practical benefit beyond any interest earned: it creates a small amount of friction before spending it, and makes it easier to see progress toward a goal at a glance.

General things worth understanding about savings accounts:

- Interest is typically calculated on the balance and paid periodically; the exact rate and how often it is paid depends on the specific account.
- Some accounts may have limits on the number of withdrawals or transfers before a fee applies - it's worth checking an account's specific terms.
- A savings account is generally intended for money that might be needed within a relatively short time horizon (months to a couple of years), as opposed to long-term investing, which carries different risk and time considerations.

This is general educational information about how savings accounts commonly work. It is not investment advice, and it is not a comparison of specific interest rates or a recommendation to move money between accounts - for decisions like that, a customer should review their own account's terms or speak with the bank directly.
